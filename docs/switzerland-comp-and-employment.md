# Evaluating a Swiss job offer

A practical orientation for reading offers and comp in Switzerland. It is what
the German modes do not cover: Swiss-specific employment and pay mechanics.

> Not legal or tax advice. Figures are approximate and change yearly; verify
> against current cantonal tables and your own situation before you rely on them.

## Gross, net, and "x13"

Swiss salaries are usually quoted as gross. Two things change the real number:

- **13th month salary.** Very common. A salary may be paid in 13 instalments,
  so "x13" of a monthly figure, or as 12 with a separate bonus. Always ask
  whether a quoted annual number is x12 or x13, and whether the 13th is
  guaranteed or discretionary.
- **Payroll deductions** come off the gross (see below).

## Deductions from gross (employee side, approximate)

- **AHV / IV / EO** (state pension, disability, income compensation): about 5.3%.
- **ALV** (unemployment): about 1.1% up to a ceiling, less above it.
- **Pillar 2 / BVG** (occupational pension): age-banded, often roughly 7 to 18%
  of the insured salary, typically split with the employer. It lowers your net,
  but it is your retirement money, and a more generous employer split is real
  compensation worth comparing.
- **Health insurance is NOT deducted by the employer.** It is mandatory and
  individual (KVG), paid directly to an insurer, roughly CHF 300 to 500 a month
  per adult depending on canton, model, and deductible. Budget it separately.

## Tax

Federal, cantonal, and communal income tax stack, and rates vary a lot by
canton and commune (Zug and Schwyz low, Zurich moderate, Geneva higher). Use a
calculator (for example comparis.ch) for a real net estimate.

- **Quellensteuer** (tax at source): if you hold a B, L, or G permit, the
  employer withholds tax directly. Above roughly CHF 120k taxable income you
  also file an ordinary return. C-permit holders and Swiss citizens file
  ordinary returns and pay separately.

## Permits

- **B**: residence permit, renewable. Common for new arrivals.
- **C**: settlement / permanent. No work restrictions, ordinary taxation.
- **L**: short-term.
- **G**: cross-border commuter (Grenzgänger).
- EU/EFTA nationals move freely; non-EU hires are quota-limited, so confirm the
  employer is set up to sponsor.

## Time off and notice

- **Vacation**: legal minimum 4 weeks (20 days). 5 weeks is common in tech.
- **Notice periods** (Code of Obligations): during probation (Probezeit,
  usually 1 to 3 months) often 7 days; after that, commonly 1 month in year 1,
  2 months in years 2 to 9, and 3 months from year 10, unless the contract sets
  something else. Check the contract.
- **No federal minimum wage.** A few cantons and cities (including the city of
  Zurich) set their own.

## Compensation reality check (senior systems / infra, Zurich)

Rough, role- and company-dependent, from public sources like Levels.fyi:

- Base at non-FAANG Swiss employers commonly lands around CHF 130k to 180k.
- Total compensation at big-tech and well-funded scale-ups runs higher, often
  CHF 200k to 350k+ for senior, more at staff/principal, mostly via stock.
- "Senior" titles map to different internal levels across companies, and the
  level (not the word in the title) drives pay. Ask which internal level an
  offer maps to.

## Questions worth asking before you commit

- Is the quoted salary x12 or x13?
- What is the Pillar 2 (BVG) contribution split?
- Is there stock (RSUs), and what is the vesting schedule and refresh policy?
- Which internal level does the role map to, and what is the band?
- Relocation support, and help with permit and health-insurance setup?
